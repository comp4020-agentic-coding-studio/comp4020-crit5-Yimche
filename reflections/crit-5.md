# Crit 5

## What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?

The moment the no-tutorial sensor went red on my own prose. I had written the
narration carefully, believed it was clean, and a check I had built an hour
earlier caught instructional wording I could not see myself. That reframed the
whole week. The durable output of a session with an agent is not the diff that
passes; it is the check that will catch the next regression, including a
regression in my own future writing. Once I saw that, the em-dash problem stopped
being something to police by eye and became a sensor wired into the build, and
the house-style rule went into `CLAUDE.md` so it survives into next week's repo.
The work moved fastest when I stopped supervising output line by line and started
building the backpressure that supervises it for me.

## What did this work change about who I want to be as a software developer?

I want to be a developer whose judgement lives in the harness rather than in
vigilance. Re-prompting until something passes is cheap and forgettable; encoding
what "good" means into a check that outlives the session is the skilled move, and
it is the one I want to reach for by default. The other habit that stuck is
trusting the running artifact over the read code. The opening-screen affordance
looked correct on the page and was broken in the browser, and I only knew because
I watched it load. I would rather be the kind of engineer who mounts the thing
and times it than the kind who is confident from reading, because this week the
reading was wrong and the watching was right.
